# The Structure of Voynichese: What the Scholarship Implies About Its Nature

A note on the central tension: the Voynich text exhibits some statistics that look strongly **language-like** (Zipf's law, long-range word clustering, semantic-network structure) and others that look strongly **un-language-like** (very low conditional entropy, rigid slot-morphology, near-binomial word lengths, line-position anomalies). Every major hypothesis is an attempt to reconcile this split. The radiocarbon date is the only truly settled fact; everything about *meaning* remains contested.

## 1. Montemurro & Zanette (2013) — information-theoretic / semantic-network finding

- **Paper:** Marcelo A. Montemurro & Damián H. Zanette, "Keywords and Co-Occurrence Patterns in the Voynich Manuscript: An Information-Theoretic Analysis," *PLOS ONE* 8(6): e66344.
- **URL / DOI:** https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0066344 · doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0066344
- **Finding (peer-reviewed):** Content-bearing words are not uniformly scattered but **cluster across long text spans**, carrying information well above random/shuffled sequences. At the optimal scale (~600–800 words) the measured information content falls **between English and Latin**. They extracted significant **co-occurrence / semantic networks** from the ~100 most informative words, and found morphologically similar words co-occur (a "connection between form and meaning"). Thematic sections show measurable relationships (Herbal and Pharmacological sections closest).
- **Status — finding vs. interpretation:** Peer-reviewed measurement of language-like large-scale word organization. The authors *hedge*: the structure is "compatible with" real language and "limits severely the scope of the hoax hypothesis," but they do **not** claim decipherment or proof of meaning. Contested by hoax-side researchers (Rugg, Schinner), who argue generative processes can mimic such statistics.
- Critical discussion: https://hydeandrugg.wordpress.com/2013/06/23/the-montemurro-and-zanette-voynich-manuscript-article-a-detailed-discussion/

## 2. Stolfi — word grammar / core-mantle-crust paradigm

- **Author:** Jorge Stolfi (IC-UNICAMP, Brazil), c. 1997–2000.
- **URLs (verified live):**
  - Word grammar / crust-mantle-core: https://www.ic.unicamp.br/~stolfi/EXPORT/00-EXPORT/00-06-07-word-grammar/
  - Prefix-midfix-suffix, A vs B: https://www.ic.unicamp.br/~stolfi/EXPORT/00-EXPORT/97-11-12-pms-AxB/
  - OKOKOKO note (fine structure): https://www.ic.unicamp.br/~stolfi/voynich/Notes/017/Note-017.html
  - EXPORT index: https://www.ic.unicamp.br/~stolfi/EXPORT/00-EXPORT/
- **Model:** Nearly every Voynichese word parses as **soft prefix + hard midfix (kernel) + soft suffix** (e.g., *qoteedy* → qo- / -tee- / -dy). The 2000 refinement recasts this as nested concentric layers: **core** (gallows {t p k f cth…}) inside **mantle** (benches/e-sequences {ch sh ee}) inside **crust** ({d l r s n…}). The grammar accounts for ~96% of tokens.
- **Implication:** Words are highly constrained and **slot-structured / paradigmatic** — most valid words are generated by filling a few ordered slots. Strikingly, the grammar *could* produce ~10²⁰ word types but the manuscript uses only ~5,000–8,000, so the real text occupies a tiny, clustered corner of the permitted space. Often read as evidence of a **constrained, combinatorial generation process** rather than straightforward phonetic transcription. Stolfi himself presents it descriptively; the mantle layer is the least certain part.

- **Papers / URLs:**

Bostius had died; colleagues published the letter, and Trithemius was branded a liar or a demon-summoner. - The work was placed on the **Index Librorum Prohibitorum** and removed in **1900**. ## 1. Hildegard of Bingen (1098–1179): Lingua Ignota and Litterae Ignotae

**This is the single strongest genuine medieval precedent for a deliberately constructed vocabulary paired with an invented alphabet.**

### Established facts
- Hildegard, the Benedictine abbess of Rupertsberg, created the **Lingua Ignota** ("unknown language") and **Litterae Ignotae** ("unknown letters"), datable to roughly **1150–1158**.
- The **Lingua Ignota** is a **glossary of approximately 1,000–1,011 nouns** (most counts cite ~1,011), arranged hierarchically: it begins with words for God and angels, descends through human beings, then animals, plants, and so on. It is essentially a constructed *vocabulary*, not a full grammar — the words are typically embedded in Latin/German syntactic frames.
- The **Litterae Ignotae** is an alphabet of **23 invented letters**, omitting j, v, and w. It also includes symbols for "et" and "est" derived from **Tironian notes** (the Roman shorthand system). Visually the letters show loose resemblances to Latin, Greek, and possibly Hebrew/Aramaic forms.
- The constructed words show **German and Latin morphological influence** (e.g., characteristic use of "z"), grammatical gender tracking the Latin/German equivalent, and euphonic/trochaic endings.

### Where it survives (manuscript evidence — precise shelfmarks)
- **Riesencodex** (the "Giant Codex" / "Chain Codex"): **Wiesbaden, Hessische Landesbibliothek, Hs. 2, ff. 461v–464v.** This is the primary witness, a near-contemporary compilation of Hildegard's works assembled around the time of her death.
- **Berlin manuscript** (formerly the *Codex Cheltenhamensis*): **Berlin, Staatsbibliothek, MS Lat. Quart. 674, ff. 57r–62r**, a later copy (the secondary witness dated c. 1220). It entered the Berlin State Library in 1912 and famously passed through several hands (including Kaiser Wilhelm II).
- Documented *uses* in Hildegard's own corpus are small but real: the antiphon/song **"O Orzchis Ecclesia"** splices Lingua Ignota words into Latin, and **two letters (c. 1153–1154)** use the unknown letters — possibly to conceal the recipients (monks at Zwiefalten and a bishop).

### Scholarly interpretations of purpose (hypothesis, not settled fact)
There is **no consensus**. Competing scholarly readings include:
- **Mystical / Adamic-language theory**: an attempt to recover the perfect, prelapsarian language of Adam, or the language of virgins in heaven — i.e., a *redemption* of fallen human language so that the holiness of all created things is made manifest.
- **Cryptographic / private-convent use**: a semi-secret register for her community.
- **Glossolalia-adjacent / visionary play**, and a substitute for Greek to underscore her divinely inspired authority.

The author should treat all of these as interpretations; the only firm fact is that Hildegard never explained its purpose in surviving texts.

**1. Prefix–midfix–suffix decomposition (1997).** Stolfi split the EVA alphabet into two classes:
- **soft letters:** {q a o y d s j m n r l i}
- **hard letters:** {t k p f ch sh cth ckh cph cfh e}

Almost every Voynichese word parses as a **soft prefix + hard midfix (kernel) + soft suffix**. Classic example: **qoteedy → qo- (prefix) + -tee- (midfix) + -dy (suffix)**. Any slot may be empty. A word made entirely of soft letters (no hard kernel) has an ambiguous prefix/suffix split and is called a **unifix**. The midfix is one or two main hard letters each optionally followed by e's.

**2. Core–mantle–crust "word grammar" (2000).** A refinement into three *nested* concentric layers (a context-free grammar), corresponding to the idea that a word's "density profile is a single unimodal hill":
- **Core** (innermost) = gallows + benched gallows: **{t p k f cth cph ckh cfh}**
- **Mantle** (middle) = benches and e-sequences: **{ch sh ee}** (the "pedestals" plus e's)
- **Crust** (outermost) = **{d l r s n x i m g}** (plus q-/o- material at the edges); the "circle" letters a, o, y attach as modifiers.

So a word is built as nested optional slots, crust → mantle → core → mantle → crust, the core sitting at the center. This is essentially the prefix-midfix-suffix model recast as symmetric nesting rather than linear affixation. **OKOKOKO** was Stolfi's intermediate name for the fine combinatorial structure (alternating element types, roughly "O-K-O-K..." building blocks); the crust-mantle-core word grammar "incorporates and refines the OKOKOKO paradigm and the crust-core-mantle decomposition."

**Coverage / generativity:** The 2000 word grammar accounts for roughly **96–96.5% of all text tokens** (about 88% of labels). Strikingly, it is a very *loose* generator: it can produce on the order of ~10^20 possible word types, yet the manuscript actually uses only ~4,500–8,000 distinct word types — i.e., the real text fills a tiny, highly clustered corner of what the grammar permits.

### What it implies about the script

- Voynichese words are **highly constrained and slot-structured**: characters occupy fixed positions/roles (which layer they live in and in what order), not free combinations.
- Word formation looks **paradigmatic and productive** — most valid words can be generated by filling a small number of ordered slots from limited character repertoires, much like a positional/combinatorial template rather than ordinary natural-language morphology.
- This rigid positional regularity (plus the A-vs-B "dialect" differences Stolfi documented, e.g. B's heavy use of -dy, A's -ol/-or/-om) is often read as evidence that the text was **generated by a constrained mechanical/combinatorial process** rather than being a straightforward phonetic transcription of a natural language — though Stolfi himself presents the grammar descriptively and the mantle layer is acknowledged (e.g., by Zandbergen) as the weakest, least certain part of the paradigm.

## 1. Leon Battista Alberti (1404–1472) and *De cifris / De componendis cifris*

**ESTABLISHED FACTS:**

- Alberti wrote a short treatise on ciphers, properly titled **_De componendis cifris_** (often cited as *De cifris* or "De Cifra"). It was composed **c. 1466** — most scholarship dates it to 1466/1467. The Italian Wikipedia entry, drawing on the critical edition, gives "1466 ca." and notes he wrote it within roughly a year to eighteen months of being commissioned. (https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_componendis_cifris — encyclopedic; https://www.cs.trincoll.edu/~crypto/historical/alberti.html gives 1467 — university/scholarly). The c.1466–67 range is the safe, defensible statement.

- **Context of composition:** It arose from a conversation in the Vatican gardens with **Leonardo Dati** (Dato), an apostolic secretary to Pope Paul II, who asked Alberti to study secure ciphers and the decryption of intercepted messages. Alberti's own framing connected the discussion to the newly arrived movable-type printing press. (https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_componendis_cifris; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberti_cipher — both encyclopedic; corroborated by Trinity College — scholarly.)

- **The cipher disk (the device):** Two concentric disks on a common pin. The outer/fixed disk (*Stabilis*) carried an ordered plaintext alphabet (20 Latin uppercase letters — excluding H, J, K, U, W, Y — plus the numbers 1–4 for codebook references), 24 cells total. The inner/movable disk (*Mobilis*) carried a *mixed/scrambled* alphabet for ciphertext. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberti_cipher; https://www.cs.trincoll.edu/~crypto/historical/alberti.html.)

- **The formula / mechanism (why it is polyalphabetic):** Encryption begins by aligning an index letter on the movable disk against a chosen letter on the fixed disk, establishing one substitution alphabet. Crucially, **during enciphering the operator periodically rotates the inner disk to a new setting** — signaling the change by inserting an uppercase (key) letter into the ciphertext. Each rotation produces a different substitution alphabet. This makes the mapping of plaintext to ciphertext **one-to-many** with **mixed alphabets and variable (irregular) periods** — the first true polyalphabetic substitution. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberti_cipher; https://www.telsy.com/en/leon-battista-albertis-cipher-disk/ — specialist/industry.) Alberti also described **superencipherment of a numeric code** (the disk's 1–4 digits feeding ~336 numeric code groups) — i.e., enciphering a code, a second major innovation. (https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_componendis_cifris.)

- **Why "father of Western cryptography":** The title is attributed to historian **David Kahn** in *The Codebreakers* (1967). Kahn credited Alberti with **three "firsts"**: (a) the **first Western treatise on cryptanalysis** (frequency analysis), (b) the **invention of polyalphabetic substitution**, and (c) the **invention of enciphered code (superencipherment)**. (https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_componendis_cifris attributes these three primati to Kahn; the Kahn epithet itself is reported by https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberti_cipher and https://www.telsy.com/en/leon-battista-albertis-cipher-disk/.) Note: this is a **historiographical judgment**, not a fact about Alberti — it is Kahn's framing, widely adopted. The polyalphabetic disk defeated frequency analysis, the only attack then known, which is the substantive basis for the claim.

**CRITICAL DATING / PRINTING DISTINCTION:**
- **Composition: c. 1466.** **First printing: 1568** — over a century later — when **Cosimo Bartoli** translated it into Italian as *"La Cifra"* within the *Opuscoli morali* collection, Venice. (https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_componendis_cifris; corroborated by the Trinity College page, which states the treatise "was never printed in the 15th century.") It circulated **only in manuscript** (about 15 surviving manuscript copies are reported — encyclopedic, https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_componendis_cifris) for ~100 years.
- This is the key point your analysis flags: **Alberti's work predates *printed* cryptography by roughly half a century.** The first *printed* cryptographic book was Trithemius's *Polygraphia* (printed 1518). Alberti is the founder of the *manuscript* treatise tradition, not the print tradition.

### Interpretation (distinct from the measurement)

The low h2 means characters are **highly predictable from the preceding character** — i.e., glyphs are placed in rigidly constrained positions within words (strong "paradigmatic"/positional structure). The interpretive implications, on which sources broadly agree:

1. **Against a simple substitution cipher of a natural language.** Monoalphabetic substitution preserves entropy exactly (the bigram plot would be identical to the plaintext, just relabeled). Since Voynichese's bigram structure is drastically more constrained/gappy than any plaintext, simple substitution of a known language is effectively ruled out.

2. **Verbose ciphers / abbreviation expansion don't easily rescue it.** Zandbergen notes such processes tend to *lower* entropy further, so they can't bridge Voynichese back up to natural-language statistics; they make it harder, not easier, to reconcile with a plaintext source.

3. **Toward a constrained/constructed or unusually-encoded system.** Candidate explanations remaining: a constructed system, a verbose/complex encoding, highly redundant text, or a real language with very different vowel-consonant patterning. Lindemann & Bowern's specific proposal: the rigid within-word character placement "may indicate the loss of phonemic distinctions from the underlying language," and they note resemblance to some tonal languages (e.g., Venda, Min Dong) that also show low conditional entropy from strong positional constraints.

## Voynich Manuscript and Zipf's Law: Findings

### 1. The established finding: Voynich IS Zipfian

It is well documented that the Voynich Manuscript's word ("vord") frequencies follow a Zipfian rank-frequency distribution — an approximate power law where frequency is inversely proportional to rank, the same pattern seen in natural languages. This is one of the headline statistical reasons the text "looks like" language rather than gibberish.

- **Gabriel Landini (2001)**, "Evidence of Linguistic Structure in the Voynich Manuscript Using Spectral Analysis," *Cryptologia* 25(4): 275–295. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/0161-110191889932 . Reports multiple statistical properties (including spectral/Zipf-type behavior) common to natural language; concludes the text is not a random collection of characters. (Landini also wrote a separate, unpublished note "Zipf's law in the Voynich Manuscript.")
- **René Zandbergen's voynich.nu** is the standard reference site. The word-statistics page (https://www.voynich.nu/a4_word.html, section 4.8) explicitly attributes the Zipf analysis to Landini, though the page itself only stubs the summary. The analysis intro is at https://www.voynich.nu/a1_intro.html . Note: voynich.nu does not itself lay out a full Zipf derivation — it points to Landini.
- **F.M.J. Lang's page**, "Zipf's Law and Word Length Distributions" (https://www.fmjlang.co.uk/voynich/VoynichZipf.html), states it plainly: *"The word frequency distribution follows Zipf's law, which is a necessary (though not sufficient) test of linguistic plausibility."* It shows the real MS and a computationally generated (state-transition-table) version produce *almost identical* Zipf plots — which itself demonstrates the weakness of the test.
- **Montemurro & Zanette (2013)**, "Keywords and Co-occurrence Patterns in the Voynich Manuscript: An Information-Theoretic Analysis," *PLOS ONE*. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0066344 . A stronger pro-language result than Zipf alone: it finds long-range word-clustering and semantic-network structure (information content at scales matching English/Latin), arguing for "a genuine message."
- Popular write-up: IFLScience, "The Voynich Manuscript Appears To Follow Zipf's Law" (https://www.iflscience.com/the-voynich-manuscript-appears-to-follow-zipfs-law-could-it-be-a-real-language-79136). Reports near-perfect linear Zipf fit (R² ≈ 0.99) but also flags the unusual binomial word-length distribution.

### 2. The crucial counterargument: Zipf is a WEAK test

Zipf compliance is **necessary but not sufficient** for natural language. Many random or meaningless processes generate Zipfian distributions, so passing the test proves very little.

- **Wentian Li (1992)**, "Random Texts Exhibit Zipf's-Law-Like Word Frequency Distribution," *IEEE Transactions on Information Theory* 38(6): 1842–1845. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/18.165464 (IEEE Xplore: https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/165464 ; SFI preprint PDF: https://sfi-edu.s3.amazonaws.com/sfi-edu/production/uploads/sfi-com/dev/uploads/filer/a5/07/a507be1c-7232-422b-8657-d24dcf24a35d/91-03-016.pdf ). Shows that "monkey-typing" random character strings with a space character automatically yield a Zipf-like power law. The power-law shape is largely an artifact of mapping word length to rank (exponential stretched into a power law) — i.e., it arises from the ranking procedure itself, not from meaning.
- **Torsten Timm & Andreas Schinner (2019)**, "A Possible Generating Algorithm of the Voynich Manuscript," *Cryptologia* 44(1): 1–19. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/01611194.2019.1596999 . Their "self-citation" generation method — a process simple enough for a medieval scribe and producing *meaningless* strings — **reproduces both of Zipf's laws** and other key Voynich statistics. This directly supports the hoax/meaningless-text hypothesis and shows Zipf compliance can't distinguish language from a mechanical hoax. (Related earlier preprint: Timm, "How the Voynich Manuscript was created," https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.6639 / PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.6639 .) The FMJ Lang page above empirically echoes this: a state-transition generator matches the Voynich Zipf curve nearly exactly.

### Bottom line

- **Established fact (low controversy):** The Voynich word-frequency distribution is Zipfian / follows a rank-frequency power law, like natural languages.
- **Interpretation (contested):** This does **not** prove it encodes a real language. Since random texts (Li 1992) and a plausible medieval-scribe hoax algorithm (Timm & Schinner 2019) both reproduce Zipf's law, Zipf compliance is a necessary-but-not-sufficient screen. Stronger claims for genuine linguistic content rest on richer structure — long-range correlations and semantic clustering (Montemurro & Zanette 2013) — not on Zipf alone. A frequently cited tension with the language hypothesis is the Voynich's unusual near-binomial word-length distribution (few very short or very long words), which is atypical of natural language.

- **Sectional structure:** The manuscript's thematic sections show measurable linguistic relationships; the Pharmacological and Herbal sections are textually closest, consistent with their shared botanical illustrations.
- **Compatible with natural language / carries linguistic information:** Their headline conclusion is that the manuscript "presents a complex organization in the distribution of words that is compatible with those found in real language sequences," and that the evidence gives "support to the presence of a genuine message inside the book."

## 2. What the hypothesis claims

The "self-citation" (also called auto-copying / self-reference) mechanism: the scribe generated each new word by looking at a word **already written nearby — typically only one or two lines above** — and copying it with a **small modification** (adding, removing, or swapping a character/glyph group). Repeating this slightly-mutating copy process across the manuscript produces the text. Timm's arXiv paper reached this by analyzing the spatial relationships between **similarly-spelled words** (they cluster close together, which is what a copy-and-tweak process predicts).

Crucially, the claim is that this purely mechanical procedure **reproduces the manuscript's known statistical properties without any underlying linguistic meaning**:
- Zipf-like word-frequency distribution
- Anomalously **low entropy** / high predictability of characters
- Strong **word self-similarity** and tight networks of near-duplicate words
- Positional/line patterns (e.g., line-initial and line-final effects, word position dependencies)

In the 2020 *Cryptologia* paper they implement this as a concrete **generator algorithm** and show it could be executed by a medieval scribe **with no tools, tables, or grilles** — and that its statistical output matches the real Voynich text.

These are three classical Kabbalistic exegetical techniques, all using the Hebrew alphabet.

**Gematria** (numerical letter-values):
- Hebrew letters double as numerals; words with equal sums are treated as linked. The Greek antecedent is **isopsephy** (rooted in Milesian numerals and Pythagoreanism, 6th c. - Temurah rearranges/substitutes letters to derive hidden meaning. **Atbash** is its most famous cipher: a reversal substitution (first letter ↔ last: א↔ת, ב↔ש — the name *Aleph-Taw-Bet-Shin*).
- **Atbash is genuinely ancient Hebrew, not a Kabbalistic invention.** It appears *in the Hebrew Bible*: in **Jeremiah**, "**Sheshach**" (ששך) is Atbash for **Babel/Babylon** (Jer. 25:26; 51:41), and "Leb-kamai" encodes the Chaldeans (Jer. 51:1). Medieval commentators (Rashi, Radak) identified these. So Atbash predates Kabbalah by well over a millennium; Kabbalists later absorbed it into the temurah toolkit. [Wikipedia "Atbash," corroborated by the biblical citations] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atbash

**Notarikon** (acronyms/notariqon):
- Treats letters of a word as initials of a phrase, or vice versa. The term is borrowed from Greek *notarikon* / Latin *notarius* ("shorthand writer"). It is attested as a Talmudic-period interpretive method (cf. Shabbat 105a). [Jewish Encyclopedia, scholarly-reference] https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/11602-notarikon ; academic discussion: https://www.ezrabrand.com/p/where-in-scripture-is-notarikon-found
